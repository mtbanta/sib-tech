Summary

Strongest option: F, a scheduled core with Claude on top. Option F scores 3.80 out of 5 against the weighted criteria in section 2.

It is a tie, and the top four are close. Option D (run 1's pick: an always-on worker with Claude on top) also scores 3.80. Option B (the scheduled web app alone, with no Claude layer) scores 3.70. Option C (the worker alone) scores 3.65. Option A (Claude as the agent, through connectors) scores 2.75. The first four share one idea: fixed code, not a language model, places the orders, at a broker operating under the new margin rules.

Why F over D, on a tie. This is a tie-break, and it is my judgment. F becomes D by adding one part, the always-on worker, and keeping the database, the limit checks and the Claude layer. D becomes F by retiring a server that has already been built. So F is the cheaper one to be wrong about.

How sensitive this is. With all eight criteria weighted equally, D and F tie again at 3.75, and B and C tie at 3.63. With operability weighted first (30 of 100), B comes first at 3.75, F second at 3.60. If Michael decides the driver will be software only, the Claude layer has no job and B is the pick.
