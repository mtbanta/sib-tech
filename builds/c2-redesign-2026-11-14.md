# Design for approval: one request budget and one protection loop (stack C, paper only)

Asked for by Michael on 2026-11-14 (saved verbatim in `builds/build-c2-log.md`, "Michael's request of 2026-11-14 (Saturday, about 4:30 PM): the redesign, saved verbatim"). It builds on `builds/c2-proposal-shared-request-budget.md`. Nothing here is built. Branch `redesign-2026-11-14`, from `main` at `9155216` (Fly runs v22).

This is research input for a human decision, never an instruction to act. Paper only.

## 1. In one page

**Today.** Five separate counters share out Alpaca's 200 requests a minute: the governor's reserve of 80, the stuck-retry allowance (40 a minute and 15 in 5 seconds), the loss stop's 60 a minute, the mid-day close-out's 60 a minute, and the summary's floor. Each counts only itself, and each was bolted on by a different release. On top of them, a position's protection is spread over about a dozen timers and maps: the order sync's recheck time, the worker's one shared timer with its three marks (`recheckTimerAt`, `leftoverTimerAt`, `restoreAt`), two follow-up lists (lost exits, stop cancels), the stuck list, the fresh-stop list, the timed-out-stop checks, and the "not filled yet" list. Most of this week's review rounds were spent on how these pieces interact (a recheck charged but never run, a sync run but never charged, a retry crowded out by a recheck).

**The redesign has two parts.**

1. **One request budget.** Every request any part of the worker sends is counted at the single place the Alpaca client sends it, tagged with who asked. Retries, rechecks and status checks (the "budgeted" work) may only start when the budget has room for their whole try, under one cap shared by all of them, with priority classes inside it. First tries of safety work (the first sale of a position without a stop, the close-out's first try, the stop and flatten commands, the loss stop's first sale) are counted but never refused, as today. Entries keep the governor's reserve of 80 and their ceilings, unchanged.
2. **One protection loop.** Each filled entry gets one protection record with one state (protected by its bracket, stop gone, fresh stop sent, fresh stop confirmed, fresh stop not confirmed in time, exits lost, selling, stuck, closed) and one "next action" time. Live order updates, the order sync's reads and the clock move a record from state to state. One scheduler wakes at the earliest next action, asks the budget for room, and runs the action. The order sync stays as the record of truth (every 10 seconds) but no longer sells inside its own walk, and no protection work ever schedules a sync.

**What you get.** Charges always equal requests sent (by construction, not by bookkeeping). No retry, recheck or check can exceed the shared cap. A stuck sale can never hold up the order sync (Monday 2026-11-09's finding). The priorities are written down instead of falling out of which allowance a path happens to use. About 15 timers, marks and counters go (section 6).

**What does not change.** The sell gate and every sell-path check, the order gate, the governor's reserve of 80 for entries, the 100 and 150 ceilings, the loss stop's 5 second account check, the close-outs' logic, every alert text and mention, the deploy rules, and the rule that every retry loop has a limit and a backoff.

### Decisions for Michael (answer by number)

1. **The budget's size.** Recommended: **60 requests a minute and 15 in any 5 seconds**, shared by all budgeted work (section 3.3 shows why: it is the largest single allowance today, and the paths that used separate allowances almost never run at once). Alternative: 90 a minute and 20 in 5 seconds (faster retries when many positions are stuck; less room for entries while it is in use). Either is an estimate until Monday's per-class counts measure it.
2. **The priority order inside the budget.** Recommended: **P1** shares without a confirmed stop (stuck sales, a fresh stop not confirmed in time, the loss stop's re-sells, the close-outs' retries); **P2** rechecks and status lookups (the list-lag looks, unclear fresh-stop lookups). P2 may draw only while it leaves 20 of the minute for P1. This **supersedes your decision 2 of 2026-11-13 ("Stuck sale priority: keep as built")**: a stuck sale would come before a recheck.
3. **Your decision 3 of 2026-11-13 ("Charge give-back: keep as built")** is superseded: there is no give-back, because nothing is charged before it is sent. Confirm.
4. **Safety first tries.** Recommended: **counted but never refused** (as today). Alternative: limited too (a first sale would then wait for room, which can delay a sale of shares without a stop by seconds).
5. **Budgeted work and the reserve of 80.** Recommended: **budgeted work also stops at the reserve of 80, and entries give way only while a budgeted try is waiting on that reserve** (it has room in the 60 but Alpaca's remaining is too low). Entries are not held back while the 60 itself is used up, so several stuck positions for many minutes (as on Monday 2026-11-09) do not stop entries while Alpaca's bucket has room. This keeps all 80 for first tries (the stop command, the close-out, flatten). Cost: stuck, timed-out and recheck tries gain a floor they do not have today; during a load window, when entries hold the bucket near 80, such a try waits for the bucket to refill by its own size (about 4 s for a 13-request try, an estimate at 3.3 a second). Alternative: budgeted work may draw down to 40 with no give-way rule.
6. **Which retries join.** Recommended: **all of them**, including the loss stop's re-sells and the mid-day and afternoon close-outs' retries. The 2:55 close-out's retry (today it has no budget at all, per the "Rule 6 audit") joins too, in P1. Alternative: keep the loss stop's own 60 a minute and the 2:55 retry outside.
7. **Shadow first, or live.** A shadow day (counting only, old allowances in force) cannot fit a Monday mid-day deploy. Recommended: **live**, with v22 as the fallback, and Monday afternoon's per-class counts reported in the end-of-day review.
8. **How it ships.** Recommended: **two parts in one release, reviewed in order** (part 1 the budget, part 2 the loop), so that if part 2 has not passed both groups in time, part 1 can ship alone (your fallback pattern of 2026-11-13). Section 6.4 says what part 1 alone does and which removals belong to which part. Alternative: both or nothing.
9. **The behaviour changes listed in section 7.** Under your rule of 2026-11-03, approval covers only what is listed there. Approve the list, or strike items.

## 3. Part 1: the request budget

### 3.1 Counting at the one place requests are sent

Every request to Alpaca's trading interface goes through one function, `once()` in `src/alpaca-trading.ts` (the reads' three tries, the cancels' and sells' retries all call it). The budget is charged there, **just before the request is sent**, by the tag of whoever asked:

| Tag | Set by | Limited? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `entry` | the order gate | by the governor (unchanged) |
| `first` | a first try of safety work: the first sale or fresh stop of a position, the close-outs' first tries, `stop`, `flatten`, the loss stop's first sale, the start-up protection check | counted, never refused (decision 4) |
| `P1`, `P2` | the protection loop's retries and checks, the loss stop's re-sells, the close-outs' retries | yes: the shared cap and its classes |
| untagged | everything else (the order sync's reads, the account view, the loss stop's 5 s check, settings, the summary) | counted as routine, not limited (unchanged) |

The clock and the calendar are in a separate Alpaca bucket (the governor already leaves them out, `observe` in `src/governor.ts`). They are logged by tag but never counted against the budget or the 200.

The tag travels with the call through Node's AsyncLocalStorage, the mechanism `src/request-scope.ts` already uses for the timed-out tries. It already follows a call through the liquidator's one-at-a-time queue and the Alpaca client's own retries (group 1 measured the client's retries charged in the weekend build's round 1 fixes). The sell gate's queue is built the same way (`chain.then`), so the tag follows it too; a test will check it. The gate's one-second cached view makes no request, so nothing is charged when a later caller reuses it.

Because the charge is made at the moment of sending:
- nothing is charged for a request that never ran, so there is nothing to give back (`lagCharge`, `refundCharge` go);
- nothing runs uncharged, whatever started it (the leftover-timer class of rounds 6 to 8 cannot occur);
- the Alpaca client's own retries are counted like any other request.

### 3.2 A try asks for its whole room first

A budgeted try states its **most** requests before it starts, as the timed-out tries do today (`TIMED_OUT_TRY_REQUESTS`, 13). It starts only when the budget has room for that whole amount, its class leaves the higher class its share, and Alpaca's remaining figure stays at or above the reserve of 80 after it (decision 5).

Each budgeted try, its most requests, and which caps it reserves against:

| Try | Most requests | Reserves against | Source of the figure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A stuck sale's retry (ordinary) | 10 | the minute and the 5 seconds | today's flat charge (`STUCK_RETRY_COST`; at most 8 measured in the simulator, 2026-11-09) |
| A try for an entry with a timed-out stop | 13 | the minute and the 5 seconds | `TIMED_OUT_TRY_REQUESTS` (v22) |
| A timed-out stop's status check, or its cancel sent again | 1 to 2 | the minute and the 5 seconds | v22 |
| A list-lag look | 2 to 4 (the order list's pages, positions, one lookup per older entry) | the minute and the 5 seconds | v22's recheck cost |
| An unclear or confirmed fresh stop's lookup | 1 | the minute and the 5 seconds | v22 |
| The loss stop's re-sell check | at most 15 | the minute and the 5 seconds | `LOSS_STOP_RETRY_PER_CHECK` (as built) |
| A mid-day or afternoon close-out retry (at most 10 positions) | the last try's measured cost, at most 60 | **the minute only** | today's rule (`retryAllowed`, the last try's cost) |
| The 2:55 close-out's retry (one round of the whole account) | the last try's measured cost, at most 60 | **the minute only** | new (decision 6) |

The close-out retries cannot fit 15 in 5 seconds (10 sells alone are 10 requests, plus the reads and cancels), so they reserve against the minute only, as the mid-day retries do today; their burst is bounded by their own single round (at most 10 positions for the mid-day and afternoon, the account for the 2:55 retry). Everything else fits 15 in 5 seconds. The room is held as a **reservation**: the requests the try sends are counted against it as they go, and what it did not use is released when the try ends. A try is never stopped halfway for lack of room (the proposal's own risk). If a try sends more than it reserved (an extra page of open orders, say), the extra is still sent and counted, logged as `budget_overdraft`, and the next try waits for it.

The reservation is taken when the try actually starts running, inside the one-at-a-time sale queue, not while it waits in that queue, so a try queued behind the close-out does not hold room it is not using.

Without room, the try is not started and its record waits for the moment room returns (computed from the budget's own counts, never a fixed tick). Waiting for room is not a backoff step: the record's backoff advances only on tries that ran.

### 3.3 Sizes and classes (decisions 1, 2, 5)

- **Cap:** 60 requests a minute and 15 in any 5 seconds for all budgeted work together.
- **Classes:** P1 first; P2 may draw only while it leaves 20 of the minute for P1. Within a class, the record that has waited longest goes first (today's "taking turns" rule for stuck entries, kept).
- **Floor:** budgeted work runs only while Alpaca's remaining stays at or above 80 after the try. Entries give way only while a budgeted try has room in the 60 but is waiting on this floor (the governor's `admitTrade` refuses with "holding back for a safety retry"); never because the 60 itself is used up.

Why 60 is enough (builder's reading of the code, to be measured Monday): today's paths with their own allowances run almost never at once. While the loss stop is in force the order sync's sales stand aside (`lossStopInForce`), and while the mid-day or afternoon close-out owns selling the sync's sales and the loss stop's retry stand aside too (`middayOwnsSelling`, `isLiquidating`). So in practice the busy set is one of {stuck sales, timed-out stops, rechecks}, {the loss stop's re-sells} or {a close-out's retries}, and each had at most 60 before. The loss stop's 5 second account check moves out of the budget into routine (it is not a retry), so its re-sells get the whole 60 where today they get about 48.

Measured needs, from the C2 log (simulator unless named):
- 8 remainders lagging 25 to 32 s at once: peak 32 a minute, 8 in 5 s ("Michael's change to the remainder rechecks (2026-11-13, evening): built").
- 3 timed-out stops, worst minute 34 to 56 ("Weekend build, steps 1 and 2").
- the loss stop's re-sells: at most 15 a check, 60 a minute (as built).
- the mid-day retries: 60 a minute (as built); the first mid-day try, uncapped, at 20 positions used 80 requests in its minute ("Wednesday 2026-11-11 mid-day session review", light load).

### 3.4 Request math at 150 orders a minute

Facts, each with its date and version:
- Alpaca: 200 requests a minute, one bucket for account, order and position calls (measured 2026-11-02); it refills continuously, about 3.3 a second (inferred 2026-11-04 to 2026-11-06; up to 217 requests in 60 s without a refusal on Friday 2026-11-06, v10).
- A load-window entry costs about 1.6 to 1.9 requests (the order, the 5 second cancel of the unfilled ones, and its share of the reads; Wednesday 2026-11-11 mid-day review, v16).
- Routine work with the live updates connected, by the code (an estimate): the account view 2 every 10 s (12), the loss stop's account check every 5 s (12), the order sync 1 page and positions every 10 s (12), plus older-entry lookups and settings, so **about 40 a minute**. Measured earlier: 35 before the open (2026-11-04), "51 plus 0.94 per entry" (2026-11-05, before the 10 minute window), 65 to 78 on afternoons with one old entry paging (2026-11-05). Monday's `governor_stats` would turn 40 into a measurement.
- Achieved on load days: highest 119 accepted in a calendar minute (Monday 2026-11-09, v12), 138 (Wednesday 2026-11-11, v16), 132 (Thursday 2026-11-12, v19). The governor, not the ceiling, set the pace each time.

| Per minute | At the ceiling, 150 entries | At the achieved pace, about 135 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entries asked for | 150 x 1.6 to 1.9 = 240 to 285 | 135 x 1.6 to 1.9 = 216 to 257 |
| Routine | about 40 | about 40 |
| Total asked for, no safety work | 280 to 325 | 256 to 297 |
| Alpaca refills | 200 | 200 |

So **150 a minute cannot be sustained on requests alone**, today or after the redesign. The governor holds entries at the reserve, and the extra minutes above 100 come from drawing Alpaca's stock down from 200 to 80 (120 requests, at most a couple of minutes' worth). Sustained, with routine at 40, entries get about 160 a minute, so about 84 to 100 entries a minute.

With budgeted work at its full cap (the worst case, for example several stuck positions during the window):

| Per minute | Today (v22): separate allowances | After the redesign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine | 40 | 40 |
| Retries, rechecks, checks | up to 40 (stuck) + 60 (loss stop) + 60 (mid-day), no shared limit; the 2:55 retry and the unclear-stop lookups unlimited | at most 60, all together |
| Left for entries out of 200 | as little as 0 if several were busy at once | at least 100, about 53 to 63 entries a minute (entries give way only while a safety retry waits on the reserve) |

**Bursts.** First tries are not limited, so a burst is drawn from Alpaca's stock. Worst case checked, starting at the reserve of 80: a 40-position close-out's first try, 69 requests in its first 10 s (simulator, 2026-11-03, "Round 8 changes (built 2026-11-03 ...)", item 2), plus routine 7, less 33 refilled in 10 s: 80 - 69 - 7 + 33 = 37 left. With budgeted work allowed down to 40 (decision 5's alternative) it could take another 30 in those 10 s and leave 7, which is why the recommendation keeps budgeted work above 80.

**First tries still dip below 80.** On Thursday 2026-11-12 remaining stayed below 80 for 16 minutes (lowest 65), and the fast sales deepened dips the entries were making. Under decision 4's recommendation that stays possible (first tries are never refused), and is reported under your dip ruling, not failed. Entries giving way to waiting safety retries removes the retries' part of such dips.

## 4. Part 2: the protection loop

### 4.1 One record per filled entry

`src/protection.ts` (new) keeps one record per open entry that has shares, keyed by the entry's client id: its symbol, its own shares, its state, the time of its next action, its backoff (current wait and tries), what Alpaca last showed of its stop or fresh stop and its sale, and its alert state (first alert, 5 minute alert, resolution line). Everything in section 2.2's `SyncMemory` list that is about one position moves into this record.

### 4.2 States

"Source" is what moves the record; live updates are believed first, the order sync's list confirms. "Class" is the budget class of its retries and checks; every first action is `first`.

| State | Meaning | Source in | Action | Timer, limit and backoff | Class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bracket` | the bracket's stop rests at Alpaca | entry filled (live update or list) | none | none | |
| `stop_gone` | Alpaca cancelled the stop at a partial take-profit; Alpaca's order list may still show it open (the list lag) | live update `onStopCancelled`, or the list | a list-lag look (the same reads as today's recheck: the order list's last 10 minutes, positions, and a lookup by id for an entry older than that; 2 to 4 requests; one look serves every record waiting, and it reads Alpaca afresh, never the sell gate's cached view) until it shows the stop ended, then the fresh-stop try | looks at 0.5, 1, then every 2 s, for up to 30 s from the live report (today's steps, unchanged); after 30 s, the regular sync | P2 |
| `fresh_stop_try` | ready to cancel the take-profit's rest and place a fresh stop (A4) | `stop_gone` cleared, or a bare remainder in the list | the cleanup sale as today (`cleanupSale`, `protectWithStop`) | first try at once; retries on the stuck backoff | first, then P1 |
| `fresh_unclear` | the fresh stop's answer was unclear | the try's result | look up by client id | +2 s, then every 5 s; at 15 s ruling B moves it to `fresh_timed_out` | P2 |
| `fresh_live` | the fresh stop is confirmed working and covers the holding | answer, lookup, list or live update | none; a lookup every 30 s only while the list does not show it | 30 s | P2 |
| `fresh_timed_out` | ruling B: not confirmed in 15 s, never counted as protection again | `fresh_unclear` at 15 s, or the start-up check | cancel it; wait until it is known ended (live update, list, or a status check), then sell | status check every 10 s with live updates down, 30 s with them up, doubling to 60 s after a failed check; cancel re-sent on 5, 10, 20, then every 30 s, never while pending cancel (today's rules) | P1 |
| `exits_lost` | no live exit and shares held (both exits ended, a fresh stop ended, a late fill after the 5 s cancel) | live updates `onExitsLost`, `onFreshStopUpdate`, `onEntryFill` after the cancel (A5), or the list | the sale (A5's fast sale when it came from a late fill) | first try at once | first |
| `selling` | a sale was sent and not seen filled | the sale's result | wait for the fill (live update or list); the "not filled yet" closing line | 2 minutes, then back to `exits_lost` if still held (today's `AWAITING_FILL_MAX_MS`) | |
| `stuck` | a try could not sell or protect the shares: held with no visible order (hidden), held by visible orders, or refused | the try's result | the next try | hidden: 5, 10, 20, then every 30 s (10 s cap with live updates down for a timed-out stop); visible: 10, 20, then every 30 s; refused: as hidden | P1 |
| `closed` | the shares are gone | exit fill to zero (live update), or the list | the resolution line if one was owed | none | |

**Rule 6 (every retry loop has a limit and a backoff):** every retrying state above has its backoff in the table, and its limit is the budget's rate cap (60 a minute, 15 in 5 seconds, shared), which you confirmed as a valid reading of "limit" on 2026-11-13. Each record's log line says which bounds applied. The list-lag looks keep their 30 s window as well.

### 4.3 Who stands aside

Unchanged rules, now checked in one place (the scheduler) instead of in each path:
- from the close-out time, and while the 2:55 close-out, the mid-day close-out or the afternoon close-out owns selling: no selling action; records keep their state, and the close-out sells everything; the stuck 5 minute alert stands aside while the mid-day close-out owns selling (your decision of 2026-11-09);
- while the daily loss stop is in force: its own re-sells own selling; records take no selling action;
- while any liquidation runs: the loop's sales wait their turn in the one-at-a-time queue, and still give way to the loss stop's sale (`yieldToPriority`);
- only the newest open entry of a symbol is acted on (today's rule); an older one gets its resolution line.

### 4.4 The scheduler

One timer: the earliest "next action" across all records, or the moment the budget has room for the first record waiting, whichever is earlier. On waking it takes the records that are due, P1 before P2, longest waiting first, asks the budget for each one's room, and runs the actions one at a time per record. A live update moves a record at once and wakes the scheduler. Nothing in the loop calls the order sync; the sync never decides when protection work runs.

### 4.5 The order sync's new role

It still runs every 10 seconds (and at start-up), still reads the order list and positions, still updates the stored records, still closes entries, still looks up entries missing from the list and older entries. **It no longer sells or places stops in its walk.** It hands each entry's order and the positions to the loop (`loop.observe`), which moves records whose state the list contradicts (a stop ended that no live update reported, a fresh stop shown working, shares gone). This is what makes it the backstop for missed live updates, as it is today.

### 4.6 Restarts and live updates down

- **Restart:** records are rebuilt from the first sync after start, today's stored timed-out stops (control key `timed_out_stops:<date>`, kept) and the start-up protection check (kept as is; its reads are `first`). The budget's counts start empty; the reserve check uses Alpaca's remaining figure from the first answer on.
- **Live updates down:** the sync's observations drive every move; the timed-out stop's checks run every 10 s; everything else as today (the account view every 5 s, `VIEW_EVERY_NO_STREAM_MS`).
