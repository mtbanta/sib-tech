### A2. The cause

**Remainders: confirmed, with counts.** For every filled bracket whose stop Alpaca cancelled while the take-profit had filled at least one share, the stop's `canceled_at` was compared with the take-profit's first fill (`transaction_time` in Alpaca's fill activities):

So the take-profit and the stop sitting together at Alpaca is the cause, as Michael suspected, and it is not a race: Alpaca cancels the stop on the take-profit's first share, every time. About two thirds of those take-profits then finish on their own within about 2 seconds (Tuesday 173 of 242 in the records read, median 1.66 s; Wednesday 34 of 44, median 1.67 s, at most 5.8 s). The rest are the remainders the stack sells.

### A3. Option 1: entries that carry only the stop (Michael's first proposal)

**What changes.** A normal-day entry is sent with only its stop attached (Alpaca's "one triggers other" order class, `oto`, with a `stop_loss` leg, per Alpaca's documentation; not yet tried on this account). The take-profit no longer rests at Alpaca. The stack watches the live prices; when a trade prints at or above the take-profit price for a held position, it cancels that position's stop, waits for Alpaca to free the shares, and sells all of them at market through the one checked sell path (`src/sells.ts`).

**Why the stop must be cancelled first.** While the stop rests at Alpaca it holds the shares (Alpaca's `qty_available` is 0), and Alpaca refuses a second sell of the same shares; `sells.ts` also refuses any sell above the shares held counting sells already open. Alpaca's "replace an order" call can change an order's price or quantity but not its type, so the stop cannot be turned into a sale in one step. So every take-profit exit goes briefly without a stop, while the price is at or above the take-profit.

**Expected time without a stop, per take-profit exit (estimate):** notice the print and send the cancel (about 0.1 s) + the stop's cancel landing (PCG's stop: 83 ms after the request; the loss stop of 2026-11-10 cancelled 34 exits in 0.3 s) + the sale accepted (decision to acceptance median 237 ms on Wednesday afternoon) + the sale filling (Wednesday's 10 remainder sales: median 1.9 s, at most 4.8 s) = **about 2.3 s typical, about 5 s when a fill is slow, about 15 s if Alpaca holds a sale as it held PCG's**. It applies to **every** take-profit exit: 72 of 130 entries on Wednesday afternoon, against 10 sold remainders today. Remainders disappear (the stack's market sale sells the whole position).
- To turn the estimate into a measurement: one paper session with the change, measuring stop cancel to sale fill for each exit.

**Load days.** Keep today's bracket for the 1-share load entries: one share cannot partly fill, so they never leave a remainder, and at a 1 cent take-profit a market sale would often fill at the entry price or below (a print at +1 cent on the IEX feed, which carries a small share of trading, does not mean a buyer is still there), while the resting limit sells at its price.

**If the worker is down or the price feed goes stale.** The stop still rests at Alpaca, so the position stays protected; it simply has no take-profit until the worker or the feed is back. Rule: no take-profit sale on a price older than a few seconds (to be set; question 3). The 2:55 close-out, the mid-day close-out and the loss stop work as today (cancel everything for the symbol, then sell), with one leg fewer to cancel, which also removes PCG's case (a take-profit "pending cancel" for 8.7 s at 2:55).

**The missing-exit check.** It must count a lone stop as protection (today it expects both legs), and treat a position whose stop is gone with no sale of ours working as unprotected (sell at once, as today).

**Cost in requests:** 2 per take-profit exit (the stop's cancel and the sale), against 2 per remainder today (the take-profit's cancel and the sale). Wednesday afternoon: about 144 against 20, over 3 hours (estimate from today's counts); small against Alpaca's 200 a minute.

**Money-touching files:** `gate.ts` (the order sent), `alpaca-trading.ts` (the `oto` order body), a new take-profit watcher fed by the live prices (`worker.ts`, `strategy.ts`), `sells.ts` (a take-profit sale that cancels its own position's stop first), `risk.ts` (the missing-exit check), `order-stream.ts` (stop-cancel handling), and outside money: `stop-gap.ts` and the summary (take-profit exits become stack sales). **The largest change here: risky to build, test and pass group 1 (up to three rounds) in one night** with the rest of this release (estimate). And Thursday morning is a load day, where it would not apply.

### A4. Option 2: the fallback (recommended)

**What changes.** When Alpaca's live update says a bracket's stop was cancelled at a partial take-profit (`onStopCancelled` in `worker.ts`, today the start of a 3 second wait, `BARE_GRACE_MS`), the stack at once cancels the take-profit's rest, waits for the shares to come free, and places a fresh stop sell for the shares left, at the bracket's original stop price. If Alpaca refuses the stop because the price is already below the stop price (Alpaca's rule for a sell stop above the market is not yet tested on this account), it sells the remainder at market, as today. Fix A's recount stays: if the take-profit filled more while its cancel landed, the stop is for what is actually left, and nothing if nothing is left.

**Expected time without a stop, per remainder (estimate):** the live update (RIOT-514's arrived 45 ms after Alpaca's cancel) + the take-profit's cancel sent and landed (about 0.1 to 1 s on ordinary cancels; PCG's took 8.7 s at 2:55) + the stop accepted (about 0.25 s) = **about 0.5 to 1.5 s typical, about 9 s in the worst case seen this week**, against Wednesday's 6.0 to 10.4 s (median 8.1) and Tuesday's 14 cases over 10 s. The stop protects as soon as Alpaca accepts it; no fill is needed. To turn it into a measurement: the review's new line (part D) on the first normal afternoon.

**What it gives up.** It cancels the take-profits that would have finished on their own (34 of 44 on Wednesday, median 1.7 s). Those remainders then hold a stop and no take-profit. Michael picks how a remainder ends (question 1): (a) at its stop, or at the next close-out (simplest; recommended); (b) with an Alpaca "one cancels other" exit pair (`oco`: a take-profit and a stop for shares already held), which has the same first-share rule, so a partial fill repeats this fallback on fewer shares; (c) the stack's live-price take-profit sale from option 1, for remainders only.

**Other effects.** The close-out and the loss stop already cancel every open order of a symbol before selling, so the fresh stop is cancelled with the rest. The missing-exit check must count the fresh stop as protection for that position (it is not a bracket leg). The 3 second stop-gap measurement (`stop-gap.ts`) ends for these cases. Load days: no change (1-share entries never leave a remainder).

**Cost in requests:** the same as today per remainder (cancel the take-profit's rest, then one order: a stop instead of a sale); a remainder that later ends at the close-out costs one more cancel.

**Money-touching files:** `worker.ts` (`onStopCancelled`), `risk.ts` and `sells.ts` (the cleanup step: a stop instead of the sale; the held-shares check unchanged), `alpaca-trading.ts` (a plain stop sell with its own client id and the same safe retry as other orders), `order-stream.ts` (recognising the fresh stop's updates), the missing-exit check. Smaller than option 1 (estimate: a few hundred lines with tests).

### A5. Not asked for: a fast path for lost exits (Michael decides whether to include it)

**What it would change.** When the live updates show that an entry filled after the stack sent its 5 second cancel, the stack treats the exits as lost at once and sells the share without waiting for Alpaca's cancel updates or the order sync (the sale waits in `sells.ts` until the exits' cancel frees the share, 0.05 to 1.06 s this morning). Today it waits for the exits' cancel updates or the sync; APLD-81 and RKT-47 waited 6 to 15 s for a sync.

**What it would have changed this morning (estimate):** cancel to sale filled about 2 to 3 s instead of a median 4.8 s, and none of the five over 10 s (the three in the slowdown also depended on the worker's speed, which v17 addresses). Cost: none extra (the same one sale). Money-touching: `worker.ts`, `entry-expiry.ts`, `order-stream.ts`. It is the only item here that acts on Thursday's load morning.

## B. Blocked sales: would step 3 have helped PCG at 2:55?

**What step 3 is** (`builds/c2-proposal-2026-11-09.md`, "A. The stuck sale", step 3): "If after step 2 Alpaca still reports fewer shares free than held, and no open order of ours or of the bracket holds any of them, send one sell for the shares held minus the open sells counted". It sets aside the check against Alpaca's free count when nothing visible holds the shares.

**PCG was the opposite case.** At 2:55:13.113 Alpaca said all 19 shares were free (`qtyAvailable` 19), and the stack's own count said all 19 were held by a visible open sell, the take-profit "pending cancel" (`openSellQty` 19). So **step 3 as approved would not have run.** What would have acted is a different rule: trust Alpaca's free count over the stack's own count of open sells.

**Would that rule have helped? Not shown.**
- Timeline (Alpaca's records): cancel of everything answered 2:55:04.272; the stop cancelled 2:55:04.239; the take-profit "pending cancel" until 2:55:12.960; our sale created 2:55:14.760, **held by Alpaca until 2:55:29.358**, filled 2:55:30.700.
- Trusting the free count, the sale could have been sent at about 2:55:04.5 to 2:55:06 (the first look), about 8 to 10 s sooner (estimate).
- But Alpaca held our sale for 14.6 s after the take-profit's cancel had finished. Nothing in the records shows that an earlier sale would have been submitted or filled any sooner.
- Risk: it sets aside one of the two guards while Alpaca still lists an open sell for the same shares. If Alpaca's free count were ever ahead of a take-profit that then filled, the two sells together would exceed the shares held (Alpaca's no-shorting setting should refuse the second; not tested).

**Recommendation: do not build it.** One case, an unproven gain, and a guard set aside. Option 1 of part A would remove PCG's case at the close-out anyway (no take-profit to cancel). Keep collecting `sale_blocked` cases.

## C. The afternoon load window: the round 3 fix, then switched on

The round 3 findings (`builds/build-c2-log.md`, "Round 3 (2026-11-11, about 2:00 PM)") and the quick review's note, each with its concrete fix:

1. **Check after the writes; put the old list back.** Today `afternoonLoadList` (`worker.ts`) checks for a cancel, the pause and the stop, then waits on two database writes (the new list, the names), then switches the strategy and the price feed and posts, without checking again. A `go` or `stop` handled during those writes is missed (probes G and H). Fix: keep the old list; after the writes, check the cancel, the pause and the stop again; if any changed, write the old list back and change nothing else (no strategy or feed switch, no post). Between that last check and the in-memory switch there is no wait, so nothing can slip in.
2. **An accurate reply for a cancelled screen still finishing.** Today a `load afternoon` sent while a cancelled screen is still running is told "already running" and nothing follows. Fix: say "The earlier screen was cancelled and is still finishing. Send `load afternoon` again in a minute or two." (or start a fresh screen as soon as the old one ends; question 6).
3. **Cancel before the stop's save.** Today `stop` and `flatten` save the stop flag first and cancel the pending window after, so a finishing screen can see the stop during its save, reply "Still paused; `go` resumes a normal afternoon", and the stop reply loses its cancel line. Fix: cancel the pending window first, then save the stop.
4. **Tails that depend on the reason.** Today every failure line ends "Still paused; `go` resumes a normal afternoon." Fix: with the stop on, "The stop flag is on: `resume`, then `go`, for a normal afternoon"; with the pause already ended, "Trading has already resumed"; otherwise the current tail.
5. **A guard for a 50-message command read.** The command read takes at most 50 messages (`discord-bot.ts`, `limit=50`). If a read during the screen returns all 50, more commands may be waiting, so the window is not decided in that pass; it waits for a read that returned fewer than 50.
6. **A flag check inside `finishAfternoonLoad`** (`commands.ts`, the quick review's note): it returns at once, changing nothing, unless `load afternoon` is switched on, as a second guard.

Plus the repo tests the reviewers asked for: each probe G and H as a test (a `go`, and a `stop`, held during the writes: the list, the feed and the strategy unchanged, no post), the reply in fix 2, the stop reply keeping its cancel line, each tail, the 50-message guard, and the flag check.

**Switching it on** means `main.ts` passes `loadAfternoon: true`, so the command works as in CLAUDE.md's standing requirement: during the mid-day pause, while flat, before 1:30 PM Central; the 75 dollar cap, up to 150 orders a minute (Michael's ruling, this window only) until the 2:00 PM last entry.
