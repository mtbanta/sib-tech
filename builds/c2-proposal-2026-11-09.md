## A. The stuck sale

**Today:** a sale that finds no shares free waits up to 6 seconds a round for 3 rounds (about 30 seconds), sends nothing, and the order sync, which runs one at a time, waits for it. With four stuck symbols a single sync took about two minutes. Every other check queued behind it: remainder sales on other symbols waited 35 to 124 seconds instead of about 5, and the lost-exit quick sync could not run.

**Proposed, in four steps:**

1. **A stuck symbol stops holding everything else.** When a round finds the shares held, not free, and no open order visible that would explain it, the attempt ends after that round instead of waiting out three. The symbol is put on a short retry list (try again after 5 seconds, then 10, 20, at most 30) and the sync carries on with the other symbols. A symbol whose shares are held by visible open orders (a cancel still landing) keeps today's waits, because that case clears in seconds.
2. **Look for hidden exits by the entry's own id.** Alpaca's open-order list has not been seen to show a filled entry's resting exits. Before the first retry, the stuck symbol's entry is fetched by its client id (one request) and any exit still live on it is cancelled by its id. Today only the remainder sale passes its own exit ids; the missing-exit sale does not.
3. **If nothing visible holds the shares, ask Alpaca once.** If after step 2 Alpaca still reports fewer shares free than held, and no open order of ours or of the bracket holds any of them, send one sell for the shares held minus the open sells counted (the rule "no sell above the shares held, counting sells already open" still applies; only the second check against Alpaca's `qty_available` is set aside, for this case only). The account has Alpaca's "no shorting" setting on (`no_shorting`, set at arming and never turned off by this code), so Alpaca itself refuses a sell above what it holds: the worst case is a refusal, logged with Alpaca's own words, and the retry list continues. At most once a minute per symbol.
4. **Escalate once.** If a symbol is still held without exits after 5 minutes, one alert says so plainly and asks Michael to close it in Alpaca's dashboard (part C).

**What it costs:** step 1 saves requests (one round instead of three). Step 2 adds one request per stuck symbol. Step 3 adds at most one request a minute per stuck symbol. All inside the existing request budget and reserve; none of these are entries, so the governor's entry limit does not apply, as today for every safety sale.

**What it does not do:** it does not explain why Alpaca kept the shares held on Monday. The logging change will say whether it was hidden exits (step 2 fixes those) or Alpaca's count (step 3 tests that). If Tuesday's logs show something else, this part is revised before anything further is built.

**Risk:** step 3 sets aside one of the two guards against selling more than is held. The other guard (held minus open sells) and Alpaca's own no-shorting check remain. A test will show that a sell can never exceed held minus open sells, and that a refusal from Alpaca is handled as today.

## B. The cleanup sale never cancels a newer position's exits

**Today:** the remainder sale and the missing-exit sale are "only this symbol" sales. They cancel every open order of the symbol and sell every free share of the symbol. They decide on the order list the sync read at its start, which on Monday was 30 to 60 seconds old. So a remainder sale for APLD-107 (already finished at its take-profit) cancelled APLD-108's fresh exits, and the same happened to NCLH-42.

**Proposed:**

1. **Look again just before acting.** Right before a remainder or missing-exit sale, the entry is fetched by its client id (one request). If its exits have filled for the whole quantity, or it no longer has shares, nothing is sold or cancelled.
2. **Cancel only that position's own exits.** The sale cancels only the exit orders that belong to that entry (by their ids from step 1), never another entry's orders on the same symbol.
3. **Sell only that position's own shares.** The sale is capped at that entry's shares still held (its filled quantity minus what its exits sold), not every share of the symbol. A newer position on the same symbol keeps its shares and its exits.
4. **A newer entry wins.** If a newer entry on the same symbol exists in the stack's records, a cleanup sale for an older entry is skipped unless step 1 shows that older entry still holds shares of its own.

**What it costs:** one request per cleanup sale (52 remainder sales on Monday, so about 52 requests over the day).

**Test:** Monday's sequence replayed in the simulator: a remainder seen on an old list, the entry finishing at its take-profit, a new entry bought, then the old remainder sale. The new entry's exits stay live and its shares are not sold. Also with the old list 60 seconds stale, and with the new entry's fill arriving during the sale.

## D. The mid-day cycle

Michael's request, word for word: "Trade from the open until 10:30 AM Central. At 10:30, block entries and run a mid-day close-out (the 2:55 close-out code, not flatten) until flat. Deploys are allowed during market hours only while the account is flat and entries are paused; this replaces the no-deploy-during-market-hours rule for that state only. After a deploy, resume trading under the same day's approved limits, with the loss stop still counting from the open. A command for me to resume or keep paused. If nothing is deployed by 1:30 PM, stay paused unless I resume."

### The day, step by step

| Time (Central) | What happens |
| :-- | :-- |
| Evening before, to 8:25 AM | Limits flow as today (evening post, approval, Armed at 8:25). |
| 8:30 to 10:30 | The morning cycle: trading as today (load window 8:45 to 9:00 on a load day). |
| 10:30:00 | **Pause on**: the gate refuses every new entry ("paused for the mid-day cycle"). Entries still working are cancelled (as the `stop` command does today). |
| 10:30:00 | **The mid-day close-out** starts: the same `closeOut()` code as 2:55 (cancel every order, sell every position through the one checked sell path, wait for its own sells), path `midday`. Retried every 20 seconds until flat. |
| When flat | Discord `trading`: "Mid-day close-out done: the account is flat. Paused for the mid-day cycle." Then `commands`: the morning's line (entries, P and L, rate-limit answers) and what happens next (see below). Day record phase `midday` with `flat: yes`. |
| Flat, before 1:30 PM | **The deploy window.** A deploy (or none) while flat and paused. |
| After a deploy, before 1:30 PM | **Trading resumes on its own** under the same day's approved limits, unless Michael has sent `hold`. Discord: "Resumed after release X (deployed while flat): same limits as this morning; the loss stop counts from the open." |
| 1:30 PM, nothing deployed | **Stays paused** unless Michael sends `go`. Discord: "1:30 PM: nothing was deployed, so I stay paused. Send `go` to trade until 2:00 PM (the last entry), or nothing." |
| 2:00 PM | Last entry, as today. |
| 2:55 PM | The end-of-day close-out, as today. |

### The pause

- **A new day-scoped state, not the stop flag.** The stop flag never clears itself, carries into the next day, and while it is on the loss stop does not check equity; `resume` clears every stop, a loss stop included. So the pause is a new key for that day only (`midday_pause:<date>` in the `control` table, with `on`, `since`, `reason`, and `hold`). The gate reads it before every entry (with the stop flag) and again just before sending, as it does the stop flag today. It cannot outlive its day: the next day has its own key.
- The missing-exit check, the loss stop, the order sync and the live order updates keep running while paused, so a position found during the pause is still protected and still counted.

### The mid-day close-out (the 2:55 code, wrapped for mid-day)

`closeOut()` in `closeout.ts` holds no state and is reused as is. The code around it in `worker.ts` is built for once a day and would do harm at 10:30. So a separate mid-day wrapper:
- **Writes its own phase `midday`, never `closeout`.** Writing `closeout` would stop the 2:55 close-out, block the carried-position sale and start the evening summary at 10:30.
- **Leaves the borrowing cap alone.** The 2:55 code sets the cap to 1 when flat (`finishFlat`), and arming does not raise it again the same day; at 10:30 that would leave Alpaca at 1 for the afternoon on a day Michael approved more. While the account is flat the cap has no effect, so the mid-day close-out does not change it.
- **Its own wait for its sells:** up to 60 seconds a try (the 2:55 code waits until 2:58), then the 20 second retry.
- **Its own "not flat" handling:** if a try ends not flat, one alert mentioning Michael, then it keeps trying every 20 seconds with entries still paused; no deploy is allowed until flat (see "Deploys" below). It does not set the day's not-flat flag or silence the 2:55 alerts.
- **Restarts prices for the afternoon:** the 2:55 code stops the price stream for the day; the wrapper leaves it running (no entries can pass the pause anyway), so a `go` without a deploy trades at once.

### Resume and hold (commands)

`resume` is taken (it clears the stop flag), so two new words:
- **`go`:** end the mid-day pause now. Accepted only when the mid-day close-out has finished flat, the stop flag is off and no loss stop is in force; otherwise the reply says which. It works with or without a deploy, before or after 1:30 PM.
- **`hold`:** keep paused after a deploy (it cancels the automatic resume for that day). `go` still ends it.
- Both are owner only, `commands` channel only, safe to repeat, and also accepted while the worker is down (it reads unread commands when it starts, as today), so `hold` sent before a deploy takes effect at start-up.
- `status` shows the pause and whether a hold is set.

### Start-up while flat and paused (after a deploy)

- **Why nothing is sold at start-up:** the carried-position sale fires only when a position is held with no order of today's on record, or when the day's not-flat flag is on. After a flat mid-day close-out there are no positions and no flag, so it does not fire. As a second guard, the deploy check (below) refuses to deploy unless Alpaca shows no positions and no open orders.
- What re-runs: the price stream (after the first loss-stop check with the equity-history look since the open), the live order updates, the governor (primed from today's stored entries), the strategy (fresh). What does not: arming, the morning flow, the live list screen (all one-shot by their phases).
- **Automatic resume only after a deploy:** at start-up, if the day's mid-day phase says flat, the pause is on, no `hold` is set, it is before 1:30 PM, and the start note says "a deploy" (a new release), the pause ends. A restart without a new release (a crash, or Fly moving the machine) stays paused and posts an alert; Michael sends `go` if he wants trading.
- Same limits: the day's approved limits are read from the day record as today; the "only tightening after Armed" rule stays.
- **The loss stop counts from the open:** its baseline is the day's starting equity in the day record (Alpaca's equity at the previous close), which a restart does not change, and the morning's losses are already in the equity. Nothing to build. (One subtlety found: while any stop flag is on, the loss stop does not check equity; the pause is not the stop flag, so this does not arise.)

### Deploys during market hours

The rule "Never deploy, restart or stop the worker during market hours" stays, with one exception (Michael's ruling), written into CLAUDE.md when this is approved:
- Allowed only when **all** are true, checked by a script right before the deploy (read only, it refuses otherwise): the day's `midday` phase says flat; Alpaca shows no positions and no open orders; the pause is on; the time is between the mid-day close-out and 1:30 PM (after 1:30 only if Michael asks); the release has passed its review.
- After the deploy: the same checks as tonight's deploys (start note, Healthchecks, the resume message or the hold).
- **Healthchecks:** a deploy takes about 1 to 2 minutes; the check's grace is 180 seconds (from the build C log, not code), so a slow deploy may raise one "down" alert. Proposed: accept it, or the deploy script pauses the check first (with the Healthchecks key already in the key file).

### What a restart resets (measured in memory)

Request figures, the live-update counters and the entry-expiry counts start again after a restart, so the evening summary would show only the afternoon for those lines. Proposed for the first build: the evening summary says "restarted at 11:02 AM; request and live-update figures cover the afternoon only", and the mid-day review covers the morning. Keeping those figures across a restart can come later.

### Mid-day review

A brief in the same shape as the daily one: `builds/midday-review-brief.md` (written with this proposal), for 8:30 to 10:30 and the mid-day close-out. Run at about 10:45 AM. ### Tests (when built)

The simulator through a full day: 10:30 pause and mid-day close-out to flat (with Monday's slow cancels and a stuck sale); the 2:55 close-out still runs and the summary still waits for it; the cap unchanged after the mid-day close-out; a restart while flat and paused sells nothing, resumes after a new release before 1:30, stays paused on a plain restart, after 1:30, or with `hold`; `go` and `hold` with every refusal; the gate refusing during the pause and again just before sending; the loss stop counting the morning's losses after a restart; the deploy check refusing when not flat, after 1:30, or with open orders.

### What it costs

The afternoon starts later (about 10:31 to 11:00 instead of continuing), and the 30-symbol live list is the morning's (no new screen). The mid-day close-out costs about the same requests as the 2:55 one. On a day when the close-out cannot get flat (as Monday's stuck sales would have), the afternoon does not trade until it is flat.
